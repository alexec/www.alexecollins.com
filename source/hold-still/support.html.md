_Last updated: 11 September 2026_

Hold Still is an iPhone app that takes one photograph a day. Yesterday's frame sits faintly
over the live camera so you line the same shot up without thinking, a notification asks you
at the same time each day, and the frames stitch into a reel that gets better the longer you
keep it up. A streak counts the days in a row.

## Report a problem

Email me at [alex@alexecollins.com](mailto:alex@alexecollins.com). You do not need an account
anywhere to do it.

Say what you did, what happened, and what you expected instead. These help:

- the iPhone and the version of iOS on it
- the version of Hold Still from the App Store listing
- how many days are in the run, and roughly when it started
- for anything about the reel, whether it played in the app and what went wrong when you
  tried to save or share it
- for anything about the streak, the days you think should be in it

## What to expect

I write these apps on my own, in my own time, so this is not a support desk. I read
everything that comes in. A reply usually takes a few days, and a fix takes longer than a
reply.

## Things that are working as intended

- **You cannot add yesterday's photograph today.** A frame belongs to the day it was taken.
  Backfilling is what makes a run of photographs worth nothing, so there is no way to do it.
- **A retake replaces today's frame.** It never adds a second one. One photograph a day is
  the whole idea, and a day with two in it is a day with none.
- **A missed day is a missed day.** No freezes, no repairs, no grace beyond the one that is
  already there: the streak is alive until midnight, because a day is not over at breakfast.
  Your longest run is kept whatever happens to the current one.
- **The reel cannot leave the app in the first week.** Three frames is not a reel. The app
  waits until there is something worth handing to somebody, and longer runs give it more to
  be. Saving to Photos and sharing open on the same day, because a reel in your library is a
  reel you can send from your library.
- **The photographs do not straighten themselves.** Alignment is by eye against the ghost.
  If the reel wobbles, that is what happened, and a fixed spot to stand does more than any
  software would.
- **The camera makes a shutter sound.** iOS plays that itself on every capture and no app
  can silence it.
- **Only the reel leaves the app.** There is no sharing a single frame. A photograph pulled
  out of a run is just a photograph, and the camera on your phone already sends those.

## If the frames are facing the wrong way

The front camera is mirrored by default, so you move the way you expect while lining up.
Turning Mirror off changes the frames from that point on and leaves the older ones as they
were. There is no re-flip, so the reel will turn over on the day you changed it.

## Getting the reel out

Two ways, both open once seven days are in. **Save to Photos** puts a copy in your photo
library and it stays there like any other video you took. **Share the reel** hands it to the
share sheet and it goes wherever you send it.

The reel inside the app is built again from your frames every time something changes rather
than kept, so it is not a file sitting somewhere you can go and find. Saving is how you end
up with one that is, which matters because iOS can clear the app's working copy whenever it
needs the room.

The first time you press Save, Hold Still asks to add to Photos. That permission only lets it
put a video in; it cannot read what is already in your library. If you say no and change your
mind, the Reel tab has a way back to Settings.

## If the daily reminder never arrives

Check it is switched on in the app, then check Settings, then Notifications, then Hold
Still. The notification is made on the phone at the time you chose, so nothing about it
depends on a signal.

## Privacy

Hold Still collects nothing, makes no network connections, and keeps your photographs on
your phone. The [privacy policy](/hold-still/privacy.html) says what that means in full.
