_Last updated: 11 September 2026_

Brushwise walks you through a full clean, morning and night: water flosser, floss, a real
two-minute brush including your tongue, then mouthwash, with a toothbrush check at the end
of the week. It runs the clock, and it tells you why each part matters. It runs on the
iPhone.

## Report a problem

Email me at [alex@alexecollins.com](mailto:alex@alexecollins.com). You do not need an
account anywhere to do it, and I would rather have a short email than nothing.

Say what you did, what happened, and what you expected instead. These help:

- the iPhone and the version of iOS on it
- the version of Brushwise from the App Store listing
- for anything about a routine, which stages you had switched on, and whether it was the
  morning or the night one
- for a reminder that did not arrive, the two times you have set and whether notifications
  are allowed for Brushwise in the iOS Settings app

## What to expect

I write these apps on my own, in my own time, so this is not a support desk. I read
everything that comes in. A reply usually takes a few days, and a fix takes longer than a
reply.

## Things that are working as intended

- **The water flosser and floss stages have no timer.** They take as long as they take, and
  a clock would only rush you. Tap **Next** when you are done. Brushing and mouthwash are
  timed, because there the number is the point.
- **A brush at one in the morning counts as last night.** The day turns over at 4 am, so a
  late routine lands on the day you think it did rather than starting a new one.
- **The toothbrush check only appears once a week.** Bristles do not wear out in a day.
  Once the check has been done, it stays away for seven.
- **A stage you switched off still shows up in the advice.** Turning it off tells the app
  to stop timing it, not to stop recommending it. A full routine is still the advice.
- **The soundtrack plays with the ring switch off.** A soundtrack you chose is the point of
  pressing Start, so it plays the way music does. Run a routine in silence and the step and
  stage cues behave the other way round: they respect the switch, and they mix with
  whatever you already had playing.
- **Start goes straight into the routine without asking about the soundtrack.** It plays
  the one you picked last, because answering the same question twice a day is not a
  choice. Switch on **Ask before each routine** in Settings to pick, and hear, each one as
  you go.
- **Every claim in the app cites something.** The tips come from current NHS, ADA and Oral
  Health Foundation guidance, and each one names its source in the source code. It is
  general guidance rather than advice from your own dentist or hygienist.

## Reminders

Brushwise asks for permission to send notifications after your first routine, or when you
first touch the reminder settings, rather than on the launch screen. Two reminders a day,
at times you pick, set to 8 am and 8 pm until you change them. They clear themselves when
you open the app, so they do not pile up in Notification Centre.

If they stop arriving, check **Settings → Daily reminders** in Brushwise first, then
Notifications for Brushwise in the iOS Settings app.

## Apple Health

Turn on **Log to Apple Health** in Settings and each finished brushing is written to Health
as a Tooth Brushing event, with the time it started and stopped. Brushwise only ever
writes. It cannot read your Health data, and the permission it asks for does not allow it
to. Change your mind in the Health app: Profile → Apps → Brushwise.

## Privacy

Brushwise collects nothing, makes no network connections, and has no account. The
[privacy policy](/brushwise/privacy.html) says what that means in full.
